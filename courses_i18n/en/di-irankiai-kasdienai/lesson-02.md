# Emails, Resumes, and Everyday Writing

Writing is one of the strongest everyday AI use cases. AI can help you start from a blank page, shorten text, make the tone more polite, or express an idea more clearly.

**After this lesson you will be able to:**

- use an email template with situation, goal, and tone;
- review a resume with AI without inventing experience;
- build your own writing prompt kit.

## Email Template

```text
Write an email.

Situation: [what happened]
Goal: [what I want to achieve]
Recipient: [client / teacher / manager / colleague]
Tone: [polite, clear, calm]
Length: up to [x] words.

Do not invent facts. If information is missing, mark it in brackets.
```

## Example

```text
Write a polite email to a client saying the project will be delayed by 2 days.
Reason: additional quality review.
Goal: apologize, reassure them, and suggest a new deadline.
Tone: responsible, no blame.
```

## Resume and Cover Letter

AI should not invent your experience. But it can help you:

- phrase existing skills more clearly;
- adapt your resume to a job ad;
- shorten text;
- find weak spots.

Useful prompt:

```text
Review my resume text.
Goal: [role].
Provide:
1. what is strong;
2. what is unclear;
3. what to shorten;
4. 5 specific phrases I could improve.

Do not invent new experience.
```

## Check yourself

**1. Why does the email template say "Do not invent facts. If information is missing, mark it in brackets"?**

<details class="selfcheck" markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Without that boundary, AI fills gaps with plausible-sounding but invented details — dates, promises, reasons. The brackets show you what to fill in yourself.

</details>

**2. What can AI do with your resume, and what can't it?**

<details class="selfcheck" markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

It can: phrase things more clearly, adapt to a job ad, shorten, find weak spots. It cannot: create experience you do not have — that is lying in a document you are responsible for.

</details>

**3. Which is better: "write an email" or giving the situation, goal, and tone?**

<details class="selfcheck" markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

The second — the more context you give, the fewer generic phrases you get, and the closer the result is to your real situation.

</details>
