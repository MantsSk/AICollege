# Build Your Personal AI Workflow

This course is about practical use, not theory. Where "AI for Everyday Life" explains *how AI works*, this course hands you ready-to-use tools: concrete prompts for concrete situations. The goal is simple: save time and improve results without handing over your judgment.

**After this lesson you will be able to:**

- name 5 areas where AI is genuinely useful to you;
- use the universal prompt as your daily starting point;
- start your own personal prompt collection.

## Where AI helps day to day

- Emails, messages, resumes, LinkedIn text.
- Document summaries and explanations.
- Ideas, plans, shopping or travel comparisons.
- Learning and self-checking.
- Work task planning.
- Creative work: posts, slide ideas, image prompts, scripts.

## The core principle

AI works best as a collaborator when you give it:

```text
Goal: what do I want?
Context: what should it know?
Format: what should the result look like?
Boundaries: what should it avoid?
Verification: what should I check myself?
```

## Universal Prompt

```text
Help me complete this task: [task].

Context:
- Audience: [audience]
- My situation: [important details]
- Tone: [friendly / professional / short / warm]
- Output format: [bullets / table / email / plan]

First suggest a structure.
Then prepare a first version.
At the end, write what I should verify myself.
```

## Check yourself

**1. What five parts make up a good everyday prompt?**

<details class="selfcheck" markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Goal, context, format, boundaries, and verification — what you must check yourself at the end.

</details>

**2. Why does the universal prompt ask for a structure before the final text?**

<details class="selfcheck" markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Checking and correcting a structure takes seconds; rewriting text built on a bad structure takes long. The cheapest place to catch mistakes is the plan.

</details>

**3. What stays on your side even when AI does the work?**

<details class="selfcheck" markdown="1">
<summary>Show answer</summary>

Responsibility: verifying the facts, making the final decision, and putting your name under the result.

</details>

## Mini Project

Create your own "AI use card." Write 5 tasks you often do and prepare one prompt for each. For example:

- write a polite client email;
- summarize a PDF;
- prepare for a conversation;
- create a social media post;
- compare two options.

> **Task check:** paste your 5-task list to the mentor and ask: "Which of these tasks suits AI best, and where is the highest risk of mistakes? Why?"

> **Mentor tip:** Ask "Help me create a personal AI use plan based on my work, learning, and hobbies."
