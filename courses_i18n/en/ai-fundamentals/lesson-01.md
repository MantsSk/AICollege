# What Is Artificial Intelligence, Really?

Artificial intelligence is not one magical program. It is a broad set of methods that let computers do tasks that used to require human judgment: recognize speech, understand text, find patterns, create images, suggest decisions, and automate actions.

In this course, we will treat AI practically: not as hype, but as a new tool for work, learning, and creativity.

**After this lesson you will be able to:**

- explain the difference between rules, machine learning, and generative AI;
- name where AI genuinely helps and where it most often fails;
- run four control questions before any serious AI task.

## Three useful levels of AI

1. **Rule-based automation** follows explicit instructions: if A happens, do B.
2. **Machine learning** learns from examples: recognizing cats from many photos, or predicting what someone may like from purchase history.
3. **Generative AI** creates new content: text, images, code, audio, plans, summaries.

Modern chat assistants, like ChatGPT-style systems, mostly belong to the third group.

## What AI is good at

- Explaining a difficult topic in simpler language.
- Helping you start from a blank page: an email, plan, idea, presentation.
- Rewriting text in a different tone or shorter form.
- Comparing options and helping define criteria.
- Finding issues in text, code, or reasoning.
- Supporting learning when you ask for explanation, not only an answer.

## Where AI is weak

AI can sound confident even when it is wrong. It can:

- invent a false fact, source, or quote;
- miss an important exception;
- misunderstand your real goal if the request is vague;
- repeat biases from training data;
- be out of date if it has no access to current information.

A good rule: use AI as a fast, patient collaborator, but not as the final authority.

## A practical thinking model

When you use AI, keep four questions in mind:

1. **What exactly do I want?**
2. **What context does AI need?**
3. **How will I verify the answer?**
4. **Are there privacy, legal, or safety risks?**

If these questions are clear, AI becomes more useful and less risky.
